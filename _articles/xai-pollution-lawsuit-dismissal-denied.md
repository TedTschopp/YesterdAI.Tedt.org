A data center’s power supply can carry a legal dependency of its own. The latest xAI ruling keeps that dependency in court.

[TechCrunch’s October 31 report](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/31/judge-allows-naacp-lawsuit-over-xai-data-center-pollution-to-move-forward/) covers a federal judge’s refusal to dismiss the NAACP’s Clean Air Act case. The underlying [opinion and order](https://earthjustice.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/10313375508_6407.pdf) was filed October 30 in the Northern District of Mississippi. It concerns X.AI Corp. and MZX Tech LLC’s gas-turbine plant in Southaven, DeSoto County, Mississippi. The plaintiffs’ representatives [identify it as supporting Colossus 2](https://earthjustice.org/press/2026/court-denies-xais-motion-to-dismiss-air-pollution-lawsuit-against-elon-musks-company).

That location matters. The dispute is about the Mississippi power plant, rather than a ruling covering every xAI facility or every temporary generator.

Judge Debra M. Brown rejected the defendants’ arguments that the plaintiffs lacked statutory standing, that the turbines qualified for a mobile-source exception under Mississippi’s implementation plan, and that the citizen-suit provision was unconstitutional. The order allows the claims to proceed.

It does not impose an immediate shutdown, impose penalties or resolve the entire case. At this stage, the court evaluates whether the complaint states a plausible claim, accepting well-pleaded factual allegations under the dismissal standard. The plaintiffs allege construction and operation without the required permits; their requested remedies remain requests.

For an enterprise planning AI capacity, the consequence is a more concrete infrastructure question. Which power source supports the capacity being promised, and what evidence supports its operating assumptions? A delivery date can depend on the generation plant as well as the computing equipment.

A project team can put the facility, permit status and unresolved proceedings beside the capacity commitment it is evaluating. Legal and engineering owners can then assess the dependencies together. The ruling supplies evidence of a continuing dispute and a rejected exemption argument; it supplies no final answer about the project’s eventual operating schedule.
