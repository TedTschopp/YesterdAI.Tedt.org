A school’s decision about AI now reaches a familiar starting point for homework: Google Search.

In its [November 3 announcement](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/education/ai-learning-era-updates-2026/), Google says its revised controls will leave Gemini, Gemini Notebook, Gemini in Classroom and Google Search entirely off for K–12 student accounts until school administrators explicitly enable access. The company says it will share more specifics in the coming months before the changes roll out.

That wording creates an access-planning task for a district. Search is included among the products whose availability administrators must decide. The announcement does not yet supply the detailed configuration choices or a precise effective date, so schools still need those particulars before translating the policy into settings.

Alongside the controls, Google announced Guided Learning in Classroom globally starting November 3. Teacher-assigned activities coach students through problems using class material, while providing teachers with information about where students need support. Guided Learning in Search starts with a pilot in selected U.S. schools later in November, with broader expansion planned for subsequent months.

Those are separate stages of availability. A globally announced Classroom feature does not establish that the Search pilot is already available to every district. Google’s claims about learning benefits also remain vendor claims in this announcement.

For a school technology team, a useful first step is to list the assignments and support processes that depend on student Search access. Put the account owner, teacher and administrator beside each dependency. Then use the forthcoming rollout details to decide which access choices support the district’s teaching approach and how staff will explain them.

Teachers need a dependable route from an assignment to the tool students can actually open. Planning that route is part of the AI decision, even when the tool already feels ordinary.
