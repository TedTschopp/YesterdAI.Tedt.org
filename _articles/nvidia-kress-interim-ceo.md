NVIDIA’s new interim CEO is also its finance chief. On Nov. 12, NVIDIA named Colette Kress to lead the business while the board searches for Jensen Huang’s successor. Kress will keep the finance job she has held since 2013. The board appointed longtime director Harvey C. Jones as its first chair. [NVIDIA’s announcement](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/11/12/3401635/0/en/nvidia-announces-ceo-transition.html)

The board accepted Huang’s resignation as president, chief executive and director on Nov. 11, effective immediately, according to NVIDIA’s filing. The search will consider candidates inside and outside the company. The board said it expects operations, customer commitments and the product roadmap to continue without interruption. NVIDIA kept its Nov. 17 earnings release and call on the calendar. [Form 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000082/nvda-20261111.htm) · [NVIDIA’s announcement](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/11/12/3401635/0/en/nvidia-announces-ceo-transition.html)

A chip roadmap reaches buyers through several sets of promises. A cloud provider can reserve capacity, a server maker can commit to shipping a system, and a software vendor can certify a driver or runtime. A team may depend on all three to start a new workload. The board’s statement gives those suppliers a common expectation to work from. Each still owns its part of the delivery.

Pull out the next order or renewal and find the date your supplier has committed to in writing. Then check who handles support for the hardware and software you plan to run. Those details give you something specific to compare with whatever NVIDIA says about its roadmap on Nov. 17.
