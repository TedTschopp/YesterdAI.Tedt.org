Fractile said on Nov. 11 that it had raised $720 million to develop and ship its first inference chips and systems. Thrive Capital, Founders Fund, Lightspeed and Redpoint led the Series C. The United Kingdom’s Sovereign AI fund and the British Business Bank also participated. The government confirmed both investments and described the full round as worth more than £500 million. Its announcement left the two public investors’ individual contributions undisclosed. [Fractile’s announcement](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/we-are-delighted-to-share-that-fractile-has-ugcPost-7526285169943625747-n5p9/) and [the UK government’s release](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/sovereign-ai-and-british-business-bank-invest-in-british-firm-developing-the-next-generation-of-ai-hardware)

When an AI model answers a request, its processor has to move model data out of memory as well as perform calculations. Fractile is designing hardware that places memory above the processor to improve how quickly the data can move. It is also building a compiler, the software that translates model operations into instructions the chips can run. A buyer would need both to run a familiar model on the new system. [TechCrunch’s reporting on the design](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/11/with-720m-round-britains-fractile-gets-a-leg-up-in-the-race-for-ai-chips/)

Chief executive Walter Goodwin says the company aims to ship its first chips and systems in 2027. The new round finances that work. [Goodwin’s shipment update](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7526304067573182464/)

For a team seeking a second supplier, the test comes when the hardware arrives. Try the models and request sizes you already serve, at the same level of concurrent traffic. Measure the response time for the slowest requests alongside power draw. Both determine how much capacity you can count on from a rack.
