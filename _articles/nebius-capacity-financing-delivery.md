A customer can pay to reserve computing capacity months before a data center can deliver it. Nebius’s Nov. 12 results put its financing plan beside a revised delivery schedule. The company expects $40 billion to $45 billion in 2027 capital spending, sized for more than one gigawatt of new capacity. It expects advance payments and financing backed by contracts with investment-grade customers to cover 75% to 85% of that spending. [Nebius’s results](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1513845/000110465926126008/tm2629571d1_ex99-1.htm) · [Shareholder letter](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1513845/000110465926126008/tm2629571d1_ex99-2.htm)

A customer contract helps Nebius finance construction and equipment. The schedule for using that equipment still depends on the facility. Nebius says it expects to deliver more than half of the New Jersey capacity contracted with Microsoft by the end of 2026, with the rest now scheduled for the second quarter of 2027. Its data-center partner began retiring temporary generation ahead of a move to permanent power, and Nebius says that reset the delivery schedule. [Nebius’s capacity update](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1513845/000110465926126008/tm2629571d1_ex99-2.htm)

For a buyer, the spending plan describes the scale Nebius intends to build. The New Jersey timetable shows why the site and its power milestones matter to any contract for future capacity. If a project depends on a cluster coming online next spring, ask how the supplier verifies the date, what portion of the order will be ready then and what other site could take the workload if power arrives late. Put those answers on the same calendar as the project that needs the machines.
