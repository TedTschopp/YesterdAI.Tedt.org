A job reduction is a decision about how a company will operate. SUSE’s management is making that decision with AI and automation in its explanation, while the evidence available to outsiders leaves the productivity calculation open.

[The Register reported on November 2](https://www.theregister.com/software/2026/11/02/suse-to-cut-one-in-ten-employees-because-of-ai/5306950) that CEO Dirk-Peter van Leeuwen told staff SUSE would reduce its global workforce by approximately 10%. The publication says it saw the internal email. In the quoted memo, he describes changing workflows, lower operational overhead and flatter reporting lines, and says demand for some existing roles has diminished.

The report does not identify the affected roles or provide a measured comparison showing how much work AI has removed. It says affected employees will be informed by November 4; the wider fiscal 2027 strategy is due to be presented on November 16. Those dates describe the reported plan, not completed departures.

That distinction matters when another company uses the announcement in its own staffing discussion. A peer’s decision can show what its leadership intends. It cannot supply the missing measurement for your organization, with different customers, systems and obligations.

For an infrastructure team, the practical question is who will carry the work after the chart changes. A support queue still needs an owner. An unusual failure still needs someone who understands the system. A faster routine task may leave the difficult exceptions untouched.

A credible staffing proposal would connect the work removed, the work retained and the people responsible for recovery. Employees also need a way to show the judgment their job contains before a task count becomes a headcount assumption. The handover deserves as much specificity as the reduction.
