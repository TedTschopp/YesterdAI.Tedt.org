A quarterly result and a multiyear purchase commitment run on different clocks. Anthropic’s amended IPO filing puts both in view, and reading them as one bill would obscure the supplier decision.

The [November 2 amendment](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026068676/anthropicpbc-sx1ax1.htm) reports $17.0 billion in revenue and a $567 million operating loss for the three months ended September 30. The company attributes the return to an operating loss chiefly to $5.0 billion of short-term compute and $2.0 billion in noncash donation-matching charges. These are company disclosures, not a measure of a customer’s return from Claude.

The filing also describes approximately $895.4 billion in aggregate committed spending under purchase and lease arrangements entered into as of and after September 30, substantially all for compute and infrastructure. Approximately $69.8 billion is due in the following 12 months. Much of the remainder extends into the 2030s and 2040s, and contractual conditions differ. The aggregate is neither this quarter’s expense nor cash already spent.

This is a substantive financial update to the filing whose [governance structure we covered on October 27](/articles/anthropic-s1-board-control/). The earlier article remains a record of that initial disclosure.

For an enterprise buyer, the useful consequence is a more specific supplier review. Match the duration of your commitment with the service you depend on, the capacity promised and the options available if delivery changes. Revenue growth alone cannot establish future availability. A large obligation total alone cannot establish an imminent interruption.

Practitioners can make that review concrete by identifying what would have to move: prompts, stored work, evaluations and integrations. Keep the export and recovery steps testable. The question for procurement is how the company’s financing and capacity plans meet your operating needs over the same period.
