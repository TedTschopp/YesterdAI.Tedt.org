  title: 'Mostly Reading: What Open-Book Scores Measure in Document-Level LoRA Adapters'
  summary: Researchers trained LoRA adapters on 150 Wikipedia articles using Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct to investigate
    what open-book scores measure in document-level adapters. They found that open-book scores largely capture a
    reading behavior common to all adapters, as random adapters trained on different documents recovered 82% of

- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31135
  title: 'Mixing Matters: Evidence Position Bias Across Sequence Mixers in Long-Context Question Answering'
  summary: Researchers analyzed how long-context models use answer-bearing evidence depending on its position within
    a prompt, comparing Transformer, state-space, and hybrid Mamba architectures by moving a gold document among
    distractors. They found that Transformers show a beginning advantage in using evidence, which state-space models
    lack, while both favor evidence near the end. Variations in prompt design and dataset affected accuracy and
    position bias, but the sequence mixer alone was not identified as the sole cause of these effects. The study
    observed that attention concentration near initial tokens correlates with beginning sensitivity in Transformers,
    and that position information remains decodable across architectures despite differing response patterns. These
    findings suggest that effective context use depends on multiple factors including model family, task, capability,
    and prompt design, highlighting the complexity of evidence utilization in long-context question answering.
