- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25835
  title: Diffusion Language Models Change Speed Across Tasks and Languages
  summary: Researchers studied two diffusion language models across 10 tasks to understand why the computational
    cost varies during text generation. They found that tasks involving copying or lightly editing input text require
    fewer forward passes per token, while open-ended writing demands significantly more, with story writing needing
    over three times the passes of grammar correction. The cost differences relate to how much of the output is
    new relative to the input and the rarity of words used. In multilingual settings, languages with more tokens
    also incur higher costs per token. Additionally, the models' decoding effort correlates with correctness, as
    incorrect answers to the same math problems require more passes than correct ones. These findings suggest that
    diffusion models' efficiency depends on the predictability of the output and that decoding speed can serve as
    a practical signal to improve inference reliability and efficiency.
