  title: Mitigating Query Shortcuts in Chain-of-Thought with Context-Aware Decoding
  summary: 'arXiv:2610.29518v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: While Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting improves the
    reasoning performance of large language models (LLMs), recent studies have shown that final answers do not always
    depend on the generated rationales. In particular, LLMs may rely on query-specific shortcuts rather than the
    information provided by their reasoning steps. In this paper, we investigate whether context-aware decoding
    (CAD), an existing contrastive decoding method, can mitigate such shortcut behavior when applied to CoT reasoning.
    We study this application as Rationale Contrastive Decoding (RCD), which contrasts the next-token distributions
    conditioned on the query with and without its rationale, amplifying tokens whose likelihood increases due to
    the rationale. We provide a theoretical interpretation of this decoding rule via a KL-regularized reward-maximization
    objective. Experiments across five reasoning benchmarks demonstrate that RCD improves answer accuracy over standard
    CoT. Furthermore, intervention analyses using ground-truth and random rationales reveal that RCD increases the
    behavioral dependence of final answers on the rationale content. Our findings suggest that contrastive decoding
    offers a simple, training-free approach to mitigating query shortcuts.'
